# Development history · October 21–22, 2026

This is an account of development activity, not a public release record. See [Release history](../../RELEASE-HISTORY.md) for public releases.

## Simulation QA: persistent world state

Archive fixes preserved death effects across a save, stopped newer roofless saves from gaining roofs, prevented clothing updates from overflowing, and kept a stuck hollow from blocking new homes. Floor completion and dissolved communities needed world-state safeguards. The source also collected smaller issues; closure alone is not evidence every observation changed.

## Client QA: readable controls and scenes

The archive addressed inspect scrolling, roof draw order, feed sizing, selecting moving people, zoom, staged visuals, bodies, item icons, animal poses, and outdated instructions. It also repaired fresh Windows checks, wizard layout, exposed internal event names, and camera level selection for staged captures.

## Light and the recent record

The archive stopped light at walls while keeping the illuminated face, and History opened on the colony rather than burying its events behind centuries of summaries.

## Terrain and ruins at a glance

Archive art revisions distinguished pale forest ground from ordinary stone, softened misleading soil and sand patterns, and made old ruin walls look different from newly built walls.

## Simulation QA: things and people keep their histories

The archive preserved weather on load, returned unused project materials, corrected caravan cargo and lost-party accounting, and kept material or story identity intact in more item flows. Changes also addressed spoilage, meal inputs, raiders' knowledge of food, and pairings that could survive a partner's departure or death.

## Cheaper perception in crowded worlds

Perception dominated the measured cost of a fed 500-person simulation. The archive reused line-of-sight work and avoided a full crowd sort without changing the behaviors checked by the review.

## Houses grow from need, then get practical fixes

The archive let households grow rooms and use materials processed through actual work. Later fixes furnished more than the first home, kept builds away from fire sources, stopped impossible workshop proposals from repeating immediately, and discouraged moves back to the camp just left. The follow-up added a longer performance measurement; it did not establish that every computer met the budget.

## A quieter record and fairer camp decisions

The archive revised camp moves to use community hardships and years lived there, distinguished welcome guests from raiders, and made departures and choices legible. It reduced repeated record lines, gave names and tales better boundaries, let the director notice fires, and grounded portal echoes in real losses. Raiders learned of only a limited set of treasures, but later tests still found cumulative losses. Windows mirror handling was revisited again after this pass.

## Craft knowledge belongs to people

The archive merged crafts brought by experienced arrivals, attempts by someone untrained when no expert remained, and teaching or refusal based on personal histories. The same pass refined death summaries, story pacing, the cost of history checks, treasure targeting, and rot and rite wording in response to follow-up testing.

## Useful spaces grow from need

The archive added fields, stockpiles, cellars, and quarries that expand where there is suitable land. Follow-up changes addressed trapped people, unreachable newcomer arrivals, rites performed too soon, reversed daily temperatures, roof materials, fire spread and fire reporting. A timing check also received room for normal run-to-run variation.

## The camp left behind

An abandoned camp remained in the archive simulation to weather into ruins, while people could carry food onward and later visitors could wonder where they went. The merged follow-up kept a travelling craft worker from being counted as lost, distinguished gifts from barter, fixed a fire-summary plural, and capped retries during Windows mirror errors.

## Staged scenes stay out of real saves

An art-review scene could write into the normal save ring and later be loaded as a real game. The archive fix stopped staged scenes from writing to that ring; it did not retroactively clean affected saves.

## Panel order and major notices

The archive centralized which panels were open and which was on top, then added brief major-event notices above them. The related QA notes pointed out presentation problems, not all of which are demonstrated fixed by the panel change alone.

## Mood findings inform leadership design

A long run produced far more breakdowns than intended. A merged design note described prestige, dominance, and influence that people grant freely. The design connects to the larger mood work; it was not leadership gameplay or proof of a tuned breakdown rate.

## Surface-flora diversity report

The archived issue compared the breadth of the surface-flora inventory with a much larger simulation. The report closed; no implementation or release claim is established by the issue text.
