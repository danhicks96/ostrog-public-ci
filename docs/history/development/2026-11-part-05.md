# November 2026 development ledger: part 5

See [Ostrog release and development history](../../RELEASE-HISTORY.md) for the timeline and credits.

This installment covers 100 source records closed or merged November 17–18, 2026 (UTC). A development merge does not establish a public release. Issue closure alone does not establish delivery or a reason for closure.

### N05-01 · Count a growing valley without mistaking movement for births

A sapling on a heavily used path could previously grow despite the traffic. Merged changes made heavy foot traffic suppress young trees and set tree growth toward a roughly three-game-year target. Ecology reporting separated arrivals from births and included time at population caps; it also counted trees and stumps. The counts make pressure easier to observe without treating arrivals as local reproduction.

*Records: 4 closed issues; 4 merged development changes.*

### N05-02 · Build censuses that say what happened rather than what looked plausible

Merged reports counted repeated deaths and whether an earlier lesson was ignored, road incidents, sources of craft knowledge, warmth by shelter, trade destinations, and the fate of treasured possessions. Other measurements separated unseen targets from visible choices and distinguished loitering from purposeful waking hours. These are observation tools, not automatic fixes for the behavior they count.

*Records: 8 closed issues; 8 merged development changes.*

### N05-03 · Read an abandoned camp from things left behind

Strangers could once appear to know too much about where former occupants went. Merged changes let graves, scorch and wear inform an interpretation; added graves that can preserve someone's name; and made a set of graves support a sickness reading rather than a default migration story. The inference remains an interpretation, not access to the exact event record.

*Records: 3 closed issues; 3 merged development changes.*

### N05-04 · Let a quarrel, verdict or damaged home leave a social consequence

The development line gained reactions from relatives who witness a quarrel or learn of a verdict, and from owners who see their furniture destroyed. A household could notice missing food without knowing who took it, so an unseen theft might be blamed on an innocent person rather than magically revealing the thief.

*Records: 4 closed issues; 4 merged development changes.*

### N05-05 · Make births part of a family rather than an isolated event

Newborns gained links to grandparents and siblings; relatives or friends could visit soon after birth. The fertility check no longer treated a couple's hunger at one dawn instant as the whole measure of whether they were fed enough.

*Records: 3 closed issues; 3 merged development changes.*

### N05-06 · Make choices show a person's temperament

The why-view gained a place for temperament. The work then grouped changes to which orders an orderly person favors, how readily a cheerful person talks, and how much someone independent prefers wandering to the shared hearth. The combined review associated differences in behavior with actual choices rather than labels, with work and idle-time balance among the concerns it tracked.

*Records: 6 closed issues; 2 merged development changes.*

### N05-07 · Keep a death from losing the camera's attention to routine chatter

Recurring small events in one place could add up to outweigh a death. The merged attention change discounted repeats of the same kind in one location. A wild-animal death was treated as major only when a person witnessed it, keeping unseen wildlife losses from competing as major news.

*Records: 2 closed issues; 2 merged development changes.*

### N05-08 · Make travel and outside threats respect what has happened

Caravan gathering began to depend on local food and season rather than a universal berry award. A new hamlet was counted on arrival, not from everyone who started the trip; a trading route stopped assuming a town that had besieged the valley was a normal partner. A further fix discouraged renewed raids on a settlement that its scenario had already broken.

*Records: 4 closed issues; 4 merged development changes.*

### N05-09 · Summarize a life or an absence without dumping routine events

A biography view kept significant life events instead of every repetitive action. Catch Up added a summary prioritizing life events for someone returning to the valley. These were merged development changes, not proof that a dedicated panel or current public build included them.

*Records: 2 closed issues; 2 merged development changes.*

### N05-10 · Let wounds, weather and memory affect the person carrying them

Pain began to combine wounds and account for depth and treatment. Dampness increased exposure to cold, thirst and temperature reached mood, and mood and stress could affect work speed. A solitude fix restored slower social need decay for solitary people rather than clipping everyone to the same rate. Memories recorded when renewed so age and recency could be distinguished.

*Records: 6 closed issues; 6 merged development changes.*

### N05-11 · Separate seeing an event from hearing about it

Merged changes consolidated sight checks for witnesses, required actual observation for fear and deference, and separated real contact from hearing someone discussed. A wound memory kept its originating event and attacker instead of a generic impression.

*Records: 5 closed issues; 5 merged development changes.*

### N05-12 · Remove a saved field no behavior used

Development review found a place-reaction field present in saves without a reader or writer. The merged cleanup retired that unused state rather than claiming to add a place attachment system.

*Records: 1 closed issue; 1 merged development change.*

### N05-13 · Make recurring settlement reports readable and act on the cold

Merged fixes repaired wording for death causes and singular orders, distinguished people whose generated names collided, and let cold lift unfinished roofing's priority before homelessness became an evening emergency.

*Records: 4 closed issues; 4 merged development changes.*
