# Development history · October 23–31, 2026

Roadmap and simulation. This is development history, not a public release record. See [Release history](../../RELEASE-HISTORY.md) for public releases.

## Planning the release and the watchable game

An early release checklist and the watchability brief were rolled into larger work. A merged roadmap placed the design backlog into later milestones, including the proposal for an in-game updater. This was planning, not a release or a shipped updater.

## Design review: make the world easier to watch

The backlog recorded distinct needs: smoother frames; zoom, input and display access; a readable feed, inspector and director; visual depth; material and creature art; richer viewer moments; and edge cases to test. The panel-management proposal moved into the broader presentation plan. The review identified work, rather than establishing that every item was implemented.

## Design review: content and construction vocabulary

The maintainers wanted worlds to differ by drawing from a wider pool, with more materials, items, recipes, gems, furnishings, instruments, room purposes, personal tastes, and descriptions. The audit separated data and reusable behavior from hand-written scenarios and recorded the rule that people, not presets, make the valley's living places.

## Why someone feels or acts

The archive merged colder high ground, an inspector explanation drawn from a person's strongest thoughts, and walls proposed by people already wary of outsiders. Those were specific pieces of the broader environment, identity, and explanation work.

## Walls after raids and a finite quarry shaft

The archive merged a wall project driven by a community's own raid and a quarry depth measured from the original site rather than the receding ground. The standalone quarry PR closed without merging because the wall milestone carried the same fix and regression guard. A wall protects only when the ring is actually closed.

## Wounds and treatment

The archive merged tissue-level wounds and treatment, alongside fixes that helped perimeter walls close on uneven land. The design also called out undifferentiated kinds of damage; the merged milestone does not establish complete damage-type coverage.

## Combat nerve and people standing together

The archive merged combat nerve, militias, breaking bands, and consequences of a breach. The broader proposals for wildlife entering settlements and traps were also logged; this merge is not evidence every trap or wildlife case was delivered.

## Sieges, prisoners, and justice

The archive added sieges and prisoners, a cavern breach's aftermath, and leadership and justice behavior, rather than treating an attack as one repeated event. The work closed several design issues; it did not mean every long-run justice outcome was exercised.

## QA reviews: visible events, stress, falls, and provisioning

Dev reviews distinguished legitimately quiet events from missed behavior, recorded Windows and simulation checks, and raised the excessive breakdown and stress rates. The archive merged stress, falling and provisioning changes in response. The reports are not a claim that every event should appear in every run.

## Portable digests and hosted checks

The archive merged portable float handling and hosted checks that compared actual world digests instead of variable timing output. One alternative CI PR closed without merging because the merged workflow provided the same check with green evidence. A second closed without merging because reducing compile jobs could not fix a Windows loader failure and would have slowed builds.

## Announcements viewers can act on

The archive added controls for which events to announce, including batching, pausing, and jumping to a report. It addressed the problem of meaningful events vanishing into the feed.

## Feelings as a design layer

A merged design note laid out feelings as a system that could support dispositions, belief, and social consequences. Separate notes recorded shallow illness, repetitive breakdowns, and missing aesthetic needs. The design note did not deliver illness or every proposed kind of breakdown.

## Feelings, relatives, and different ways to fail

The archive merged the first round of feelings, rivals, persistent valley conditions, and a distinction between being absent and ceasing to belong. Birth and family relationships, childhood, social memory, rituals, waterside work, desire paths, and thermal hazards were recorded among the connected design needs; the round covered parts of that wider scope.

## Weather, water, and knowledge of place

The archive merged work on sleep, aquifers, cave plants, weather, herds, limited observation, different ties to communities, and varied landforms. Fluid movement and the wider cave-creature pool were broader designs; the round did not claim a complete fluids simulation or a fully populated underground.

## Fields, stone, and simulation detail

The archive merged a round on water reaching fields and materials making a real difference, with a recorded direction for distant simulation detail. These covered identifiable slices, not full irrigation, industry, or distant simulation.

## Regional geology and coal seams

The archive added variation in deep rock and coal that could be followed across a valley. Older saves kept their former default because they had no regional deep-rock data.

## Design review: more lives, industries, and histories

The closed design records covered crops, food, logistics, labor, domestic spaces, clothing, music, arts, ancient writings, manufactured helpers, genetics, learning, backstories, and stronger threads for the missing people. Treat this as the recorded wish list and design rationale, not a claim the features were added by closing the issues.

## Tales of the camp that moved

The archive replaced a circular place-name in old-camp tales with a direction and prevented young children from reporting that they remembered the abandoned home.
