# Development history · October 18–20, 2026

This is an account of development activity, not a public release record. See [Release history](../../RELEASE-HISTORY.md) for public releases.

## Third QA pass

The archive corrected the camera's settlement centre, visibility of missing people, underground collapse handling, stories attached to objects, and misleading tool or screenshot output. The prior review had shown that ancient structures could pull the camera away from living settlers.

## Scaling the simulation

The archive moved the chronicle to disk, changed how nearby things were looked up, and staggered decisions across ticks. A long run used less memory than keeping the chronicle in memory.

## Art review and larger character sprites

The archive added a way to prepare and check palette sprites with provenance, and devs redrew character builds, ages, and outfits at larger cell sizes. Scaling the old proportions directly did not read well, so the figure itself was revised.

## A valley inside a living world

The archive merged regional climate, rivers, peoples and languages, settled lands, historical figures, kingdoms and wars, then connected the local valley to that generated history. Legends made the history browsable and the wider world kept changing after arrival. Opening soaks covered the connected simulation; a failing anomaly test was repaired and downstream tests rerun.

## Industry, fragile supplies, and trade

The archive added spoilage, recipes that consume actual materials, new crafts, weapons, trade at scarcity-sensitive prices, and crafted works that record remembered events. Soaks exposed unsupported structures and a broken settlement-planning assumption: a hungry community was counting its neighbours' stores. Changes separated local stores and building decisions.

## Handbook, design decisions, and repeatable evidence

The archive merged a handbook, a peoples design brief, newcomer guidance, and a fix log that distinguishes findings from unconfirmed explanations. Census and test tooling made review evidence reproducible. These changes recorded the work and its reasoning, rather than implementing the design brief.

## Peoples, personal projects, and the deep

The archive merged work proposed by people with lived reasons, body plans, visual rigs, varied senses and schedules, authored and generated kinds, local customs and rites, stories and relationships between cultures, and settlements below the surface. Recorded events could lead to conditions such as the restless dead. Long runs passed the reported milestone gates, but at normal zoom some upright kinds still looked too similar.

## Wizard failures and fresh-checkout QA

A rejected start used to silently create a different world. The archive fix kept the wizard open with the reason, while a fresh-checkout check built the missing extension before testing scripts. A separate set of local injury files was never committed and was not an archive regression. The archive also added a check that the asset inventory matched its evidence.

## Quiet beats, clearer silhouettes, and a fed stress test

The archive distinguished genuine missing events from legitimately quiet runs, added a wooden spear and reaction to raids, and introduced a silhouette check for character kinds. A fed 500-person workload exposed repeated memory scans; revised indexing reduced the reported cost while preserving the tested memory decisions.

## Homes, shelter, and moving camp

The archive recorded actual wall materials, let people propose homes after sleeping outside, and allowed a threatened community to dig protected storage. A distinctive forest followed from underground terrain, and difficult conditions could make a community propose moving camp or break up.
