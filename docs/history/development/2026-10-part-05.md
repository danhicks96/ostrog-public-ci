# Development history · October 23–31, 2026

QA, water, and the player view. This is development history, not a public release record. See [Release history](../../RELEASE-HISTORY.md) for public releases.

## Hands-on release checks

The maintainer verified that the Windows package launched and that the viewer worked when exercised by hand on a real monitor. Both tracker blockers closed. Those confirmations were about the package and viewer, not a record of a public release.

## Faith, observance, and the deep

The archive merged observance and shrines, devout people leading gatherings, omens, relics, pilgrimage and heresy, and stories about a child taken below or returning later. The related design asked for guilds, societies and worship affecting divine power. Record these as a mix of concrete merged behavior and broader design, not a claim that all were implemented.

## Wildlife activity, carving, and arrival shock

The archive QA pass corrected animal behavior or its reporting, a carving-work problem and the lack of a response to arrival. The burrower's apparent idleness was a diagnostic gap, not evidence that it never moved. A standalone PR closed without merging because the main archive implementation already counted digging as an activity.

## Water can break into cuts and spill from catchments

The archive merged lakes with depth, transfer of surface water into nearby excavations, and flooding driven by rain, storms and snowmelt. A tunnel-flooding PR closed without merging because the main implementation addressed the same case more completely, including lowering the source's level.

## Siege behavior and walls that actually close

The archive directed attackers toward standing opponents and corrected siege endings. Further work repaired perimeter plans that left a gap at an existing building and made defenses follow changes in where a community lived.

## Justice with visible consequences

The archive made exile and long-running family feuds reachable, and fights could leave wounds and consequences for descendants. Later work made wrongful judgments distinguishable in the feed and history.

## Hazards and rare events

The archive refined how rock determines usable materials, and QA changes made frost, limb injury and shelters reachable in more conditions while preventing repeated break-in or strike reports. A question about the curse's target was logged separately; the review does not show that it changed.

## Documentation and evidence checkpoints

Archive-only docs recorded priorities, dev responsibilities, open merge gates, soak evidence and exact checkpoint distinctions. The evidence notes covered such work as water behavior, workshop recovery, wound inspection and ledge rescue; they did not ship those features themselves.

## Mouse-first controls and the visual plan

The archive recorded the graphics and creature plan and merged the mouse-first interface slice: a button bar, favourites, and direct inspection of people and blocks. The plan's remaining art and creature work was not part of that interface change.

## Body size without multi-tile occupants

The archive merged effects of size on hunger, blows, loads, growth, and fitting through places. A later two-by-two occupancy PR closed without merging because the maintainer decided creatures must occupy at most one tile. The reported tests do not turn the rejected approach into shipped behavior.

## Portable development checks when hosted runs could not start

The archive changed hosted checks to manual while they could not start and added a separate development runner with check results, including Windows build output for checkpoints. Timing was improved and merge evidence switched to that runner. These were workflow changes, not gameplay.

## No meal yet

A raw sentinel in inspection looked like a very large meal date. The archive changed the inspector to say that a person had never eaten until an actual meal occurred.

## Rescue, retreat, and medical access

Archive fixes let people retreat from flood danger, made it possible to reach and tend someone cut off below, kept displaced builders nearer a reachable side, and helped an entire community get down from a ledge. These addressed failures where people died despite help or a route that should have been available.

## Bounded memory and less redundant search

The archive reduced save-time memory, distributed heavy searches while preserving determinism, and reused work for shared stores, sight and water. A duplicate perception PR closed without merging because the merged shared-store change provided the same behavior and the older branch conflicted; its focused evidence was kept for reference.

## Food, water, shelter, and thirst

The archive let poor shelter or lack of supplies change priorities, then added thirst, drinking and wells. Devs corrected cases where people wandered or idled despite unmet basic needs.

## A long fall does real harm

The archive made falls respect solid rock and tied injury severity to fall distance rather than treating a very long fall like a lesser one.

## More familiar and unfamiliar plants

The archive added more wild plants and crops, including similar-looking plants people could learn to avoid. This put a specific slice of the wider flora proposal into the simulation.

## Readable terrain, caves, and settlement art

The archive merged quieter ground textures and more grass variants, additional object and material treatment, readable unlit cavern surfaces, and sharper shoreline and settlement props. It also preserved successful art direction. One terrain draft closed without merging in favor of a narrower replacement; a shoreline mockup closed without merging once production assets moved to separate changes.

## Small fixes with better evidence

The archive limited pursuit decisions to knowledge a character actually had, counted time spent starving in long-run checks, improved lamp placement by doors, and sent exhausted people toward beds they knew.

## Cold, clothing, and interrupted meals

The archive made the risk of a cold journey part of the decision to take it, stopped unusable clothes from being equipped, and fixed interrupted meals that restarted after freezing. These were targeted responses to a foraging-and-cold finding.

## Personal observations and lessons

A merged design added a direction for observation and lived lessons. An older handoff PR closed without merging because the newer merged workflow and design contract replaced it and rebasing would revive stale checkpoint text.

## Give orphaned workshop plans a chance

The archive let people take over orphaned workshop orders rather than repeatedly proposing a workshop that never appeared.

## Water quality changes decisions

The archive made sea water salty and mixed waters brackish, modelled rot fouling water and water clearing with time and flow, gave bad water a disease consequence, and made people avoid water they knew was unsafe unless desperate. This was the water-quality work, separate from the earlier addition of thirst.

## Wounds visible in inspection

The archive made wound layers visible in the person inspector, so the viewer could understand more than the fact of an injury.
