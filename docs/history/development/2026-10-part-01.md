# Ostrog development records: October 2026, part 1

This record follows outcomes in the development tracker, not release dates. Closing a tracker entry is not proof that a change shipped. The condensed [release and development history](../../RELEASE-HISTORY.md) explains the larger milestones and separates development packages from public releases.

## October 14

### Checkpoint and restore procedure

The first milestone made recovery testable: verify a mirrored checkpoint, verify its repository bundle, and restore a clean copy before running the normal checks. It was marked complete in development.

### Repeatable simulation and protected saves

The early save milestone required matching results when rerunning the same seed or resuming from a snapshot. It also called for rejecting a tampered manifest and falling back to an older save slot. It was marked complete in development.

### The game client connects to the simulation

The first client integration milestone recorded a native simulation bridge loading on Linux and a Windows library building. A pipe-based connection remained the fallback. The record does not establish a packaged Windows launch.

### A staged visual target for the valley

A staged scene brought together people, buildings, vegetation, water, the underground, lighting, and work animation at two viewing scales. The visual guide recorded the result of that review; it was not a review of an integrated release build.

### Navigation across levels

The navigation milestone covered three-dimensional routes, links between levels, fast reachability checks, and rebuilding affected regions after a map change. It called for measuring route-search throughput and was marked complete in development.

### Autonomous needs and limited knowledge

A seeded check called for twenty inhabitants to meet basic needs for ten game-days without ignoring reachable supplies or choosing food they did not know about. The tracker marked the check complete; this is not a survival guarantee for other worlds.

### The first glance review

An early integrated review used screenshots and short footage to assess the camera, event feed, and away summary. It recorded the seed and simulation tick so the views could be revisited. The tracker marked the review complete.

### Contested work and accountable supplies

The work milestone tested prerequisites, competing claims on the same supplies, expiry of abandoned reservations, recovery after interruption or a vanished target, and material accounting. It was marked complete in development.

### Memories that change choices

A focused scenario compared a person who remembered a poisoned kitchen after sleep with a control who did not. The milestone also called for bounded memory storage and for strong memories to affect opinions. The tracker marked it complete.

### Rotating saves and recovery checks

The save milestone covered a rotating series of saves, manifest validation, migration from an earlier format, interrupted writes, and parity with uninterrupted play. It also called for measuring autosave interruptions while exercising viewer controls. The tracker marked it complete.

### Communities that trade and split

The community milestone covered roles, shared work, cohesion, newcomers, and trade between settlements. A split needed a recorded cause rather than appearing without explanation. The tracker marked the milestone complete.

### Inheritance, learning, and customs

The milestone separated inherited traits from learned behavior. It called for evidence across three generations and for customs whose origins could be traced to events and whose practice could influence later choices. The milestone was marked complete in development.

### Excavation and the underground

The underground milestone covered layers of rock, caves and ore, construction below ground, and the discovery and lighting needed to make the results intelligible. Its plan treated reduced simulation detail as optional, to be justified by measured need rather than assumed. The milestone was marked complete in development.

### Journeys with lasting outcomes

Caravans and their encounters became part of the development plan for the world beyond the valley. The milestone called for outcomes that survived the journey in possessions, memories, and the chronicle, including a record when a caravan did not return. The milestone was marked complete in development.

### A chronicle tied to real events

The History and camera milestone aimed to keep history entries tied to recorded events, respect what the watcher could discover, and use footage to tune how long the camera stayed with a subject. The milestone was marked complete in development.

### Wildlife stalls were hard to measure

Normal animal movement and genuinely stuck animals could receive the same warning because the activity timer was not reset when animals changed behavior. The record also noted that a wandering label could outlast the path. The tracker marked the report complete; the warning itself was not evidence that wildlife had stopped moving.

## October 14–17 · early development and QA

### Guided checkpoint testing

A guided test suite was added to the archive so devs could exercise the same behaviors after each checkpoint and distinguish bugs from intended simulation outcomes.

### Headless capture, discoveries, and the chronicle

The archive patch let save-only runs save and made unsupported headless screenshots fail explicitly instead of hanging or silently disappearing. Rain and snow changes reached the feed and chronicle, and different discoveries could share a location. The devs also replaced a suspect callback, but Windows launch errors needed a retest. Craft-event emission was reported alongside weather; this patch did not show that craft events were emitted.

### Shutdown diagnostics

Dev testing found renderer allocations reported at shutdown. The report said the count did not grow with runtime. The issue closed; this record does not establish that the warnings were fixed or that memory accumulated during play.

### Learned crisis response

The archive merged individual fire response and care for injured settlers based on acquired techniques and beliefs, replacing settlement-wide orders. People could learn through observation and practice; fire and its aftermath became more visible, and vivid memories could survive sleep.

### Ground transitions

The archive replaced hard square boundaries between neighboring ground materials with irregular edges. Built floors, cave floors, ores, and some level changes deliberately kept hard edges. Cached chunks avoided a per-frame cost, although a software-rendered chunk redraw took longer.

### Readable movement and layers

The archive merged smoother walk cycles and distinct motions for chopping, tending, or light work. Layers were aligned by age and build so clothes and hair stayed on the character; animals and the bear gained more readable silhouettes and motion. The north-facing bear report was closed after the redraw without a separate patch.

### Reference for dev testing

The archive guide gathered player controls, launch behavior, save documentation, a simulation overview, and generated tables. This was documentation only. It exposed several control and presentation issues tracked in the next review pass.

### Input, lamp, sound, and asset-import fixes

The archive fix separated the director toggle from camera pan, kept follow active on plain mouse motion, opened arrival for a chosen seed, aligned outfits by name, and kept burning lamps on their lit frame. Cooking and dousing gained sound mappings, along with other audible feedback; reports of missing mappings are grouped here. The screenshot workflow checked, retried, and refused incomplete imports, while the client gained a texture fallback. This did not claim the engine import crash itself was fixed.

### Terrain and art become reachable

Archive changes let climate, moisture, and stone shape the biomes, then attached more ground, plants, and goats to generated worlds. Follow-up work addressed winter plant art and the tendency for founding sites to look the same. The docs-only closure recorded that deep water was still uncommon and CI did not check asset reachability; this was not a claim that every unused asset had shipped.

### Adaptive music prototype

Nine procedural cues were merged into the archive, with seasons, time of day, underground context, and tension determining how music changed. Signal checks and some live transitions were recorded. Listening quality, crossfades in play, and the underground change were not established by the reported review.

### External pressure arrives

The archive added hungry outsiders who could camp, raid, negotiate, or leave, and wildlife that could threaten isolated settlers. The earlier reports described the absence of a meaningful outside force; the merged change introduced concrete threats rather than promising every form of attack.

### Buildings take shape over time

The archive added marked sites, rising walls, roofs, and visible construction progress. Support, snow, and fire made roof safety a consequence of construction and maintenance, rather than an instant placement result.

### Objects carry histories

The archive merged records of who made, used, wore out, or inherited an object. Repeated use and deeds could give exceptional tools a name and a reputation, so the story arose from use rather than assignment.

### Old places, study, and lore

The archive first generated abandoned settlements, graves, roads, and surviving objects. Study then let curious settlers recover crafts that improved real work. A final pass kept factual history separate from rumor, which could travel and change with its tellers. These three changes together closed the original scope.

### Creatures with visible habits

The archive added hibernation, migration, scavenging, pack hunting, the rare pale stag, and reprisals after repeated attacks. Animals now offered behaviors to recognize, not just different sprites.

### Overlapping trouble becomes a story

The archive merged world pressures that leaned on existing systems based on each deity's motives. The feed connected concurrent troubles only where causation was visible and recorded when hard times eased.

### The thin place

The archive merged an anomaly lane in which long-running portal changes exposed dreams and traces rooted in prior events. Settlers could study or ward the area. This was the initial anomaly implementation, not evidence of a public release.

### Development and test regressions

Two closed reports flagged divergent work that removed tests or features and a stale injury test that could not compile after an interface change. These are recorded as QA failures; the source reports alone do not establish what fixed them.

### Scattered survivors are people, not scripted outcomes

The archive change gave missing people the same simulation rules as everyone else. Searches could find only what existed, rumors spread through people who had seen them, and old saves could be converted.

### Roadmap priorities recorded

The archive merged revised scope and priorities into the development plan and tester reference. This change recorded intent only; it did not implement the features it listed.

### World setup and hearth evenings

World setup moved ahead of the history pass so the selected deity could affect a valley's actual past. A five-page wizard exposed the valley, starting people, and shared beliefs. The archive also gained hearth storytelling and group identities derived from evolving convictions.

### Second QA pass: truth, controls, and accounting

The archive fixes kept unseen survivors out of live presentation, aligned history dates with the arrival calendar, stopped animals from showing human traits, and corrected duplicate wording. Reloads reset former world state; wizard controls, terrain variety, camera controls, audio captures, and Windows checks were repaired. Raiders removed food through the ledger and left without becoming newcomers; raw meat could be cooked. Missing memory measurements no longer masqueraded as success. The away-summary change used one game hour, not the earlier documented ten minutes.

### Underground risks become visible

The archive added depth-dependent rock and ores, named caverns, fungus light, underground creatures, and roof spans that could fail without support. Shoring became something characters could learn. This replaced a randomly chosen one-tile cave-in rule.
