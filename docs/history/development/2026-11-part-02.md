# November 2026 development ledger: early records, part 2

See [Ostrog release and development history](../../RELEASE-HISTORY.md) for the timeline and credits.

This installment covers a further 87 source records closed or merged from November 1 through November 11, 2026 (UTC). A merged development change does not prove public release; an issue closure does not establish why it closed.

### N02-01 · How a settlement remembers and organizes itself

Closed design discussions separated what happened from what people remembered or later inferred, and proposed institutions that could develop around shared needs instead of being assigned in advance. Portal-connected places were treated as neighbors with their own pressures and resources, not timed waves. Discussion also favored curses with traceable causes and room for mistaken suspicion. A merged documentation update recorded direction, including the preference to keep those curses organic; it did not implement these proposals.

*Records: 5 closed issues; 1 merged development change.*

### N02-02 · Terrain, plants and animals respond to where they live

A cave entrance used to be something people mostly found by digging. A merged change cut rare hillside mouths only when they led to a real cavern and could be traversed in both directions. Other merged changes placed cave fauna according to habitat, selected a limited set of wild plants for each world, let soil and other surface materials affect crop growth, and let repeated foot traffic compact loose ground while preserving what could grow back. The closed ecology briefs describe the broader direction; the merged work covered these specific slices.

Follow-up work compared fluid and ground options before changing behavior, and explored a save-compatible way to move more content into data. The merged slices let settlements dig channels to bring water to dry fields, added depth-specific cave animals, let slopes carry loose rock, and made elevation affect temperature and frost. Additional strata meant a deep shaft could pass through different bands of rock. These slices did not implement a full fluid or lava simulation.

*Records: 3 closed issues; 14 merged development changes.*

### N02-03 · Watch a settlement change without playing director

The viewer roadmap called for a repeatable launch, manual checks on a monitor and a way to watch long stretches of change. One merged feature captured a fixed view at opt-in intervals, bounded how many captures it kept, and provided review playback and comparison. This was a first timelapse implementation, not a claim that the full viewer roadmap or release checks were done.

*Records: 2 closed issues; 1 merged development change.*

### N02-04 · Make aboveground and underground scenes clearer

The graphics brief noted that individually readable sprites still looked like separate catalog pieces when assembled into a settlement. Merged art changes made vegetation and creature silhouettes easier to distinguish, improved underground material and seepage cues, and added presentation for combat, fire and unusual effects. These changes were visual or audio presentation, not new underground ecology or combat rules.

*Records: 1 closed issue; 3 merged development changes.*

### N02-05 · Keep validation evidence narrower than the claims

The settlement reliability plan brought cold exposure, repeated failed work, ground sleeping and abandoned defenses under one review. Two merged documentation updates recorded siege and active-wound evidence, distinguished it from unverified treated-wound coverage, and tracked which changes still needed checks. Recording the evidence did not remove those gates or change the simulation.

A later merged batch tested awkward scenarios and fixed an inhabitant left inside a tile when the last way out was walled up. A four-seed, 600-game-day run and the accompanying definition-of-done wording update recorded what the measurements showed without promoting the outstanding criteria to verified.

*Records: 1 closed issue; 5 merged development changes.*

### N02-06 · Rebuild navigation regions without allocating scratch space every time

The merged change kept navigation's region-building scratch space around and reset it before rebuilding. A guard deliberately contaminated the old data to check that it could not influence the next rebuild. The traversal and region rules were unchanged.

*Records: 1 merged development change.*

### N02-07 · Keep the work board and review gates current

The development line gained a work-package board that separated ownership, acceptance and verification. Follow-up documentation recorded completed slices, distinguished evidence from remaining work, and kept the next checks visible. An audit found two tracker items covered by existing work, one with a remaining gap and one requiring human viewer testing. A separate handoff was closed without merging; its closure reason is not established by the available record. These were coordination and evidence changes, not new game behavior.

*Records: 11 merged development changes; 1 proposal closed without merging.*

### N02-08 · Reduce the expensive spikes without changing the result

Measurements on a 500-person world separated ordinary perception cost from bursts of work after events such as freezing or large excavations. Merged changes parallelized the read-only part of perception while keeping deterministic results, replaced repeated haul and bed scans with lookups, reused chunk walkability for rebuilding navigation, and remembered unchanged failed searches until navigation changed. A later document recorded the comparison and corrected the population target; the documentation itself did not change simulation behavior.

*Records: 9 merged development changes.*

### N02-09 · Make the Windows installer usable before the game is installed

Work moved large downloads into streamed staging, exposed download and installation stages to a responsive status window, and let an empty directory start an initial install. A regression test checked that the launcher defers an update during a save write without touching the installed files. Supporting changes separated private staging from the intended download route, documented signing and rotation, and addressed a first-install package that was staged but never made available by the tool. These merges cover installer and publishing tooling; they do not prove that a package was publicly released.

*Records: 11 merged development changes.*

### N02-10 · Give upper storeys a reason and make them legible

The development line gained autonomous reasons for building upstairs, such as limited space, a growing household or a learned local practice. Ground-floor alternatives remained available, and construction depended on basic settlement security. The viewer made a person's storey readable relative to the terrain, including below ground. The closed umbrella also covered cave entrances and habitat work; this entry records the distinct upper-storey delivery and its evidence audit.

*Records: 2 closed issues; 3 merged development changes.*

### N02-11 · Watch and inspect with the mouse

The viewer work added readable cause lines to event panels, feed-rate control and first-run help. Block inspection reported only facts backed by the simulation; a click-only run on a real monitor found control problems and informed fixes. A freely positioned or edge-snapping bar moved to later work rather than being counted as complete. A follow-up audit identified delivered and missing pieces, and a first set of activity indicators made ongoing work easier to read at a glance.

*Records: 1 closed issue; 6 merged development changes.*

### N02-12 · Expand food storage inside a completed wall

The development fix stopped existing stockpiles outside a completed defence ring from drawing new storage growth farther outside. It addressed where stores expand, not the separate question of which crops the settlement should grow.

*Records: 1 merged development change.*

### N02-13 · Write down what an opt-in playtest may report

The merged design work proposed making any playtest report optional, minimal and inspectable, with a public register explaining why each field is collected. Approval of that document is not evidence that collection was implemented or enabled.

*Records: 1 merged development change.*

### N02-14 · Measure and prototype a faster world renderer

The frame-time harness used a real client window and separated time spent in the renderer, simulation and presentation. An opt-in path then drew people and animals in batches from a snapshot, leaving the established layered path as the default. Handoff notes explicitly kept the prototype default-off pending its full frame comparison.

*Records: 4 merged development changes.*
